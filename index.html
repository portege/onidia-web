So, what is it?

A friend for your desktop,
not another app to manage.

You go about your day. She stays at the bottom of the screen, doing her own thing — wandering, stretching, napping. Every now and then she says something. If you have something to ask, she listens, thinks, and talks back.

01

She lives there

A small always-on-top window with per-pixel transparency — only Onidia and her speech cloud are visible. No taskbar entry, no window decorations, no desktop clutter. She walks back and forth, blinks, sways her tail, and bobs gently. Double-click and she hops. Grab her and carry her around.

02

She talks back

Type at her and she answers in comic speech bubbles, in her own voice, with a face that matches the mood of what she says. Speak into your mic and she transcribes it. Four AI backends, from a local Ollama model to Gemini, Bedrock or OpenRouter — your call, switchable with one flag.

03

She does things

Celebrations, confetti, hearts, a lightbulb over her head, a disco ball, a guitar, a jump rope, a skateboard, a hovercar, an iron-man suit-up. She naps on your schedule, reacts when you throw her, and parachutes down if you drop her from the top of the screen.

For your tools, too

Any program on your machine can write one line to her speech pipe and she'll say it — a build finished, a deploy shipped, a server down. Build a green and she celebrates; a failure and she gets genuinely upset about it.

Whichever character you like

Onidia is the teal-haired chibi girl. Kama is the boy in the red hoodie. Both have their own palette, art and phrase list, selectable at launch. And because a character is just a Go package, making your own is a weekend, not a rewrite.

The other half

Give her a brain
and a voice.

Onidia is the body. onidia-chat is the mind: a tiny chat window, also pure Go on raw X11, that talks to a real language model — then hands every answer to the pet so she says it out loud with a matching expression.

Ask anything. She answers twice.

The reply appears in the chat bubble and is spoken by the pet, in sync with the audio. The call runs on its own goroutine, so the window never freezes — it just shows a ... bubble while it thinks. The last 20 turns stay as context, and the model is invited to tag each answer with a mood so her face matches.

Gemini Amazon Bedrock Ollama (local, no key) OpenRouter / any OpenAI-compatible
# you type in the chat window
you › what should I do about the build?
# the model answers, tagged with a mood
bot › [happy] all green - nice one!
# onidia-chat forwards it to the pet's speech pipe
pet → [happy] all green - nice one!
# ...and Onidia says it, out loud, smiling

Speaks & listens

Replies play through the Typecast API. The mic button records a take, transcribes it (Amazon Transcribe or local faster-whisper) and drops the words in the box for you to edit before sending.

Pictures too

Ask about a place or a thing and she fetches a real photo from Pixabay or Wikipedia, or has Gemini generate an illustration — and it shows up inside her speech bubble, too.

Pluggable agents

She can call real abilities: play a song, play a movie, read a story, or animate the pet. Drop in a folder with an agent.json and she can use it. No rebuild.

Checks itself first

Before the window opens, a preflight gate verifies your backend is really reachable and the environment is sane — and tells you exactly what to fix instead of failing mysteriously later.

Written, designed and tested on a Pi 5

Sixty-four bits, end to end.

Onidia and onidia-chat are pure Go, so a build is a single static arm64 binary — nothing to cross-compile on the Pi, nothing to install beside it. The release artifact is exactly that: one arm64 .deb that carries both halves. A Raspberry Pi 5 is what they were written against, designed around and tested on.

The more interesting half is what that Pi can be as the machine. On a AI HAT+ 2 — the Hailo-10H board, 40 TOPS of INT4 inference and 8 GB of RAM living on the card itself — Hailo's hailo-ollama speaks the very same ollama /api/chat dialect onidia-chat already speaks to the network. Point the provider at localhost and the model stops being someone else's computer.

Nothing leaves the Pi. No key, no account, no request to a vendor you cannot audit — pull the network cable and it behaves identically. One honest footnote: hailo-ollama rejects the tools field, so onidia-chat retires tool calling after the first reply and carries on in plain text.

How a frame is made

A tiny state machine — idle, walk, hop, drag, throw — runs a few physics steps a frame and produces a pose: bob, leg swing, foot lift, blink, arm flags, expression. The pose is rendered procedurally onto a supersampled canvas and downsampled for smooth edges, the speech bubble is composed above it, and the whole image is uploaded with a single PutImage. The window itself just moves to follow her.

Because the character is code and not a sprite sheet, adding a personality means writing a Go package: its own palette, drawing functions and phrase list, registered at startup and picked with -character. Two characters can never collide, because they share nothing but the pose contract.

XShape is deliberately not used: under XWayland, shrinking a shaped window's region can stop updates from being presented, so the plain transparent window composites correctly everywhere instead.

Worth knowing

characters/One self-contained package per personality — onidia, kama, and a template for yours.

Get her onto your desktop

One line.
That's the whole install.

One package carries both halves — the pet, the chat window, her config, and the agents that make her act. It's static Go: no runtime to provision, no toolkit, no build step.

one line — debian, ubuntu, raspberry pi os
curl -fsSL https://onidia.babeh.com/download/ondia.deb -o /tmp/onidia.deb && sudo apt install -y /tmp/onidia.deb

apt rather than dpkg -i on purpose: it resolves the audio and PipeWire packages she needs in the same step. Then put your key in ~/.config/chat-app/chat-app.ini (or just export GEMINI_API_KEY=...), start onidia before onidia-chat, and she's awake.

After the one line above. Both binaries land in /opt/onidia/bin with /usr/bin symlinks, and her bundled agents in /opt/onidia/share/agents.

first launch
# terminal 1 - the body. It creates the speech pipe and logs it.
onidia
[buddy] speech: talk to the buddy with:
  echo "hello buddy!" > /tmp/desktop-pet--0.say

# terminal 2 - the brain
nano ~/.config/chat-app/chat-app.ini     # your API key lives here
onidia-chat

# not sure this machine is ready? ask her before the window opens
onidia-chat -preflight warn

# what she can do, and what's switched off
onidia-chat -agents-list

# ...and let any program talk to her, from anywhere
echo "deploy finished, great job!" > /tmp/desktop-pet--0.say
Before you install

What she needs,
and what she can live without.

Short version: an X11 display, and a key for one model provider. Everything else is either installed for you in the same apt step, or genuinely optional.

Required — she won't start without these

1
An X11 displayNative Xorg, or XWayland on a Wayland session. $DISPLAY must be set — she draws in software straight into the X server.
2
Debian-family LinuxThe package targets amd64, arm64, armhf and armel: Ubuntu, Raspberry Pi OS and friends.
3
A key for one model providerA free Google AI Studio key, an OpenRouter key, or nothing at all if you run Ollama locally.

Installed for you — in the same apt step

♫
PipeWire or ALSApipewire-bin / pipewire-utils, alsa-utils and pulseaudio-utils. A microphone to listen from, a speaker to talk through.
●
Python 3Most of her agents — playing music, animating the pet — are small Python scripts. This is the one real dependency.
⚙
xdg-utilsSo the launchers in your applications menu can actually find her.

Optional — switch any of these on later

Whisper, so you can talk to her

Typing is the fallback; this is how she actually listens. It's a faster-whisper model, and it is not small — so it is deliberately opt-in rather than something the package drags in for you.

once
sudo apt install python3-venv
python3 -m venv ~/.local/share/onidia/venv
~/.local/share/onidia/venv/bin/pip install faster-whisper

# then in ~/.config/chat-app/chat-app.ini
# stt = whisper
# stt-whisper-cmd = ~/.local/share/onidia/venv/bin/python3

Amazon Bedrock

Only if you want her running on Claude. She talks to AWS through the Go SDK and reads the standard credential chain — ~/.aws/credentials, environment variables, or an instance role. You do not need the AWS CLI installed.

terminal
# ~/.aws/credentials
[default]
aws_access_key_id = AKIA...
aws_secret_access_key = ...

# ~/.aws/config
[default]
region = eu-central-1

# then start her with
onidia-chat -provider bedrock

Fully local

No key, and nothing leaves the machine. Ollama can be the brain and Whisper can be the ears — together that is a chat window that never touches the internet.

terminal
ollama pull qwen2:1.5b
onidia-chat -provider ollama -model qwen2:1.5b

Setup the Mind

She needs one of these to think. Pick whichever suits you — they are independent, and only one is ever needed. Each guide has the exact commands and the exact lines for chat-app.ini.

Hailo-Ollama →

With an AI HAT+ 2: run the model on the NPU, entirely on the Pi.

Gemini →

Free, no billing, five minutes. Get the key and paste it in.

Bedrock →

Run her on Claude. Create an IAM user, scope it, write the key with the AWS CLI.

Setup the Voice

Separately from the mind: every reply she writes is also spoken, in a voice you choose. She ships with a demo one, so this is only worth doing if you want her to sound like somebody in particular.

Set up Typecast →

A Typecast account, an API key and a voice id, so every reply is spoken. You can audition voices on their site before you spend a character of your quota.

Agents →

Not setup, but the same family: drop a folder in and she can do new things — play your music, control the pet, or your own script. Install, list and delete them from the one binary.
